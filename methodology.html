Research design

How the study was constructed and validated

The methodology is cumulative: each cycle contributes a classification or mapping, checks it, and passes the result to the next cycle.

Literature-driven Three iterative cycles Internal + expert validation Ordinal questionnaire data

Cycle 1

Identify adaptation needs

Uncertainty is treated as a central driver of adaptation, then interpreted through the elements that influence robotic behavior.

Starting literature

The study begins with established uncertainty taxonomies and descriptions from self-adaptive systems, robotics-oriented uncertainty work, and the Multi-Annual Roadmap for Robotics.

  • Uncertainty dimensions and taxonomies
  • Self-adaptive systems classifications
  • Robotics uncertainty and modeling
  • Multi-Annual Roadmap for Robotics

Targeted grounding

Candidate needs were checked through focused searches in Google Scholar, Scopus, and the ACM Digital Library. Queries combined robotics, adaptation, and uncertainty terms with terms specific to each candidate category.

Download the RQ1 search strings

Cycle 1 inclusion rule

A study was included if it discussed an adaptation need or strategy in robotics, or if it discussed adaptation in autonomous or self-adaptive systems while providing robotics examples or applications.

Cycle 2

Analyze classical BT features and limits

A focused review identifies studies that use, adapt, evolve, refine, or extend Behavior Trees in robotic systems.

Search source and scope

The search was run through SciVal and restricted to the robotics domain. Candidate studies were screened for adaptation mechanisms involving BTs in robotic systems.

("behavior tree" OR "behaviour tree") AND (adapt* OR evol*) AND robot*

Three extraction views

  1. Classical BT features supporting robotic control
  2. Limitations of classical BTs for adaptation needs
  3. Mappings from Cycle 1 needs to the selected studies

The final corpus contains 31 primary studies.

Cycle 3

Characterize BT enhancement approaches

The same corpus is revisited to understand how the proposed mechanisms attempt to overcome the limits identified in Cycle 2.

Type of change
Generation, extension, evolution, refinement, or a combination of these classes.
Adaptation need
The knowledge, perception, actuation, system, mission, or environment need addressed by the study.
Trigger
The condition or event initiating adaptation, together with reactive/proactive and design/runtime characteristics.
Goal
The behavior, performance, mission, safety, quality, or resource objective motivating the change.
Adapted subject
The BT, its nodes or subtrees, parameters, grammar, external model, controller, or other system element changed.
Human involvement
Whether the mechanism is manual, partially automated, or automated.

Validation

Three complementary expert checks

The final artifact is examined from the perspective of study authors and robotics/BT researchers and practitioners.

13

Author validations

Authors of 13 out of 31 primary studies responded to customized checks of the assigned limitations, adaptation needs, and technique classes.

46

Questionnaire responses

Researchers and practitioners rated need relevance, the sufficiency of classical BTs, and the suitability of four enhancement classes.

6

Expert interviews

Semi-structured follow-ups were used to clarify critical responses and explain domain-dependent judgments.

Replication path

Recreate the study in seven stages

The deposited artifacts support a traceable path from literature grounding and study selection to technical synthesis and expert validation.

1

Rebuild the candidate need set

Use the RQ1 queries and starting literature to extract uncertainty and adaptation concepts, then interpret them for robotics.

2

Reapply Cycle 1 criteria

Check definitions, robotics evidence, examples, and category boundaries using the RQ1 worksheet.

3

Rerun the BT search

Execute the SciVal query in the robotics domain and screen studies for BT-related robotic adaptation mechanisms.

4

Extract classical BT evidence

Use the RQ2 worksheets to reproduce features, limitations, source excerpts, and need-to-study mappings.

5

Reproduce technical synthesis

Classify the 31 studies by approach, technique, system, trigger, goal, adapted subject, and human involvement.

6

Reanalyze questionnaire data

Use the released response dataset, retain Likert responses as ordinal data, and treat “Insufficient expertise” as missing rather than numeric.

7

Triangulate the evidence

Compare literature mappings with author validation, response distributions, open answers, and the six follow-up interviews.
